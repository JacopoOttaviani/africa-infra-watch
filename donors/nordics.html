Where it is on record

Aid is drawn at each country's interior point, sized by seven years of commitments; the CRS carries no coordinates, so a country point is the honest place. Power units with a Nordic owner sit at Global Energy Monitor's coordinates in the map's status colours. Debt owed to the Nordic countries is off until switched on.

The five providers are summed in the bubbles; the per-provider split is in the chart below. Norfund's and Swedfund's company investments, and Norway's and Sweden's investment stocks, have no country-level open series, so the ground footprint is what GEM's owner field holds.

Aid activities: OECD Creditor Reporting System

Each of the five reports to the OECD at activity level: Danida, the Finnish foreign ministry, Iceland's agency, Norad and the Norwegian foreign ministry, Sida. Together they give __CRS_TITLES__ distinct titles for __CRS_N__ records, grants to governments, multilateral trust funds and NGOs, with climate, energy access, governance and humanitarian aid the recurring purposes. Norway's and Sweden's records are the largest.

The purpose codes are the reliable part. The descriptions often name a region or a town (__CRS_PLACED__ records mention one), but the CRS carries no coordinates, so the aid is drawn at country points and kept off the project map.

Power units with a Nordic owner

The map's power layer carries an owner field; this table is those records filtered for a Nordic company. Scatec dominates: its solar plants under Egypt's and South Africa's procurement rounds, its hydro in Uganda, Rwanda and Mozambique, and a release-and-restart history that the status column shows. Norfund and Finnfund hold stakes beside it, KLP and Frontier Energy as investors, Wärtsilä as an engine supplier that kept a plant, and Equinor in two pipeline segments.

__TABLE_ENERGY__

Country-year series: investment stock, net aid, debt

Three official series describe Nordic money in Africa one country and one year at a time, the first only in part. The OECD's FDI positions carry African partners for Denmark, Finland and Iceland; Norway and Sweden report none at this level, so their investors (Norfund, Scatec, Equinor, Swedfund) are visible only in the owner field. The DAC2a table gives net ODA by recipient, summed over the five. The World Bank's debt statistics give what each government owes the five, which is little: these are grant providers.

How to read this page

One record is
In the aid tables, one CRS activity as each of the five reports it. In the energy table, one GEM power unit, one pipeline (its segments counted) or one cable, as the map carries them. In the series, one country and one year.
Status
GEM and TeleGeography records use the map's own mapping. Aid activities have no status.
Money
CRS commitments and disbursements are in current US dollars as reported. Net ODA is current prices. FDI stock is the OECD's net position, immediate counterpart, summed over the reporters that publish African partners. Debt is the World Bank's bilateral public and publicly guaranteed stock. None of these is a project value and none is added to the map's lenders.
Location
Aid and the series are drawn at the country's interior point. Pipelines and cables are the map's routes, thinned; power units carry GEM's coordinates.
Not here, and why
  • Norway's and Sweden's investment stocks by African country are not published at the OECD's level; the national statistics publish Africa as a region.
  • Norfund's, Swedfund's, IFU's and Finnfund's portfolios are published as company lists with amounts per investment on their own sites; the site does not read them yet. Norfund's is the largest and would add Globeleq's plants.
  • The five providers' IATI files carry locations for many projects; the site does not read them yet.
  • Submarine cables: the map's cable layer names no Nordic owner on an African landing.
