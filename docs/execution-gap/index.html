The execution gap

Africa has 518.6 GW of power capacity announced or approved and 75.4 GW under construction. Of everything in the pipeline, being built or not yet begun, 87% has not broken ground; a further 228.7 GW has been cancelled, shelved or mothballed, three times what is being built. The continent's working fleet is 271.3 GW. Those are the 4,295 units Global Energy Monitor tracks across 55 countries and territories, every technology, in its release of 24 Sep 2026.

Solar is where the gap is widest and most concentrated: 234.8 GW of photovoltaic capacity is announced or approved, 45% of everything on paper, and 76% of it sits in six countries. Mauritania's 53.1 GW is 5 green-hydrogen schemes: GreenGo Energy Group (30.0 GW), CWP Global (12.0 GW), Conjuncta and Infinity Power AS (5.0 GW), Chariot Ltd (5.0 GW) and Möhring Energie (0.5 GW). Among the owners the site's lender profiles can name, Chinese-owned generation stalls more than it runs, 10.9 GW stalled against 1.4 GW operating; Gulf-owned capacity is the most active foreign-owned capacity, with 8.3 GW approved and 6.7 GW being built. And when China finances a project, AidData's records say it takes a median 3 years from the commitment to completion: 4 for an Eximbank loan, 1 for a grant from an unnamed government body.

Who owns what stalls

The 10 owner lists of the lender profiles (China, Gulf states, France, Nordic countries, Italy, Germany, Spain, Türkiye, United States and Japan) resolve a home for 391 of the 4,295 units; 1,039 units, 145.2 GW, carry no owner at all. Among the units the lists do name, China's stalled generation, 26 units and 10.9 GW, led by Musina-Makhado power station (1,980 MW), Gwayi power station (Shanghai) (1,200 MW), Zimbabwe power station (1,200 MW) and Colenso power station (1,050 MW), is many times its operating capacity of 1.4 GW; the Gulf's companies have 8.3 GW approved, 6.7 GW under construction and 5.1 GW running. The 45.6 GW under construction with named owners the lists do not resolve, and 14.5 GW more with no owner stated, say how much a proper ownership register would add.

How long a Chinese-financed project takes

AidData dates each Chinese official commitment to Africa and, where the project was finished, its completion. Of 2,487 infrastructure records committed 2000–2023, 1,577 carry both years: a median of 3 years from commitment to completion, a mean of 2.9. Transport is slowest at 4 years (264 projects), education fastest at 1 (275). An Eximbank loan takes 4 years (472 projects, quartiles 2 to 5), a Ministry of Commerce grant 3 (351), a grant from an unnamed government body 1 (286).

How to read this page

Sources
Global Energy Monitor, Global Integrated Power Tracker, release 2026-09 published 24 Sep 2026, fetched 14 Oct 2026 by fetch_sources.py gem: every power unit in Africa, with its status, technology, capacity and owner as GEM files them (CC BY 4.0). AidData, CLG-Global 1.0, Nov 2025 · to 2023, fetched 14 Oct 2026 by fetch_sources.py china: the infrastructure commitments of Chinese official lenders and donors to African recipients, with commitment and completion years, funder, sector and flow (ODC-By 1.0; the locations the site draws come from GCDF 3.0 and its OpenStreetMap footprints, ODbL, and are not used here). Both sources allow commercial use with attribution.
Stage
GEM's own statuses are folded into the site's five, by GEM_STATUS in fetch_sources.py; this page prints the result and changes nothing:
  • announced → announced
  • pre-construction, permitted → approved
  • construction → under construction
  • operating, retired → operating
  • cancelled, shelved, mothballed → stalled
“Retired” units count as operating by that rule and “stalled” mixes a cancelled project with a shelved one; GEM's status is on every unit's page.
Capacity
Each unit's capacity_mw, summed. GEM files the hydrogen schemes' later phases as “future phases” units of tens of gigawatts (the largest, Megaton Moon Hybrid Wind project, Future phases, alone is 29.4 GW); they are counted as announced, which is what they are, and tagged H₂ so the reader can see them. Nothing is weighted by ownership share or probability.
Technology
GEM's tech field (its technology type, or the fuel where the tracker has no type) folded into ten groups: PV and assumed PV are solar; onshore, offshore and the wind tracker's “Unknown” mount type (45 units, every one a wind farm by name, checked at build time) are wind; gas turbines, combined cycles, steam turbines, engines and the integrated solar combined cycles are gas and oil; subcritical to ultra-supercritical are coal; and so on. 339 units carry no technology at all; 118 of them are sorted by a word in their name (nuclear, hydroelectric, coal, wind, solar, geothermal, gas, oil) and the other 221, 64.7 GW, mostly a “power station” with no fuel, stay “not stated”.
Owners
The owner string GEM gives each unit, matched against the lender profiles' owner lists in fetch_donors.py (CHINESE_OWNER, GULF_OWNER, …): regular expressions on company names, written to find each lender's assets, not to classify the tracker. They resolve 391 of 4,295 units. A unit matching two lists counts in both (19 units); the first match colours it in the solar chart, in the order China, Gulf, France, Nordics, Italy, Germany, Spain, Türkiye, United States, Japan. In the solar chart the European lists are one colour. Known misses: Total Eren (6.3 GW on paper), HDF Energy (17.1 GW on paper) and Copenhagen Infrastructure Partners (10.0 GW on paper); “Libyan General Electric Company” matches the American list.
Hydrogen
A unit is tagged H₂ when its name or owner matches a pattern of the known green-hydrogen schemes (hydrogen, Megaton Moon, AMAN, Conjuncta, Project Nour, Hyphen, Zhero, NAYRAH, White Dunes, Tsau). The matched projects are listed in the page's data file so the tag can be checked.
The lag
AidData's commitment_year and completion_year, the difference in whole years; a record enters only with both. Quartiles by the inclusive method. The sector is AidData's, folded to the site's buckets by fetch_sources.py; the funder is the first institution AidData names, grouped as China Eximbank, the Ministry of Commerce, China Development Bank, the state commercial banks (ICBC, Bank of China, CCB, CITIC), the embassy, and “unspecified government body” where AidData could not name one. The flow is AidData's loan or grant. Because only finished projects have a completion year, the distribution is of what got done, not of what was promised; a project that never finishes has no lag.
What is not here
The AfDB, World Bank and EU finance layers on the site carry no dates, so nothing on this page uses them for speed. OpenStreetMap's construction works are not joined to GEM's units. Hypothesis 3 of the survey, that stated start dates slip, needs the next GEM release diffed against this one and is not tested. The owner lists resolve a tenth of the units; the full owner view needs GEM's Global Energy Ownership Tracker.
Reproduce
python3 build_execution_gap.py reads the two files in data/ and writes this page and execution_gap.json, which holds every table behind every chart, the rules above and the lists the caveats name.
