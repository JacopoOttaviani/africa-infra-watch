Africa's strategic corridors

Africa's strategic corridors come in rival pairs. The United States and the European Union back the Lobito Corridor to take Zambian and Congolese copper west to the Atlantic; a Chinese state company holds a 30-year concession to rebuild TAZARA and take the same copper east to Dar es Salaam. Morocco and Algeria each have a pipeline to bring Nigeria's gas north to Europe. Kenya's and Tanzania's new railways compete for the trade of Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi and eastern DR Congo, and Ethiopia's can leave by a Chinese-built railway to Djibouti, an Emirati-run port at Berbera or, one day, Kenya's Lamu.

China lends for, owns or builds pieces of 13 of the 19 corridors in this register, more than any other foreign power, and all 13 pieces it leads are in service or being built. American money is in one, Lobito; European money or ownership in six. Across 37 countries the register holds 84 pieces: 39 in service, 19 being built, 11 approved, 12 only announced and 3 stalled.

The newest pieces are mostly on paper. The railway from Lobito into Zambia has a development bank's loan but no builder; Uganda's standard gauge line has its loans approved but a contract its army chief called cancelled; the Abidjan–Lagos highway has a treaty, an authority and studies but no money to build; the Atlantic gas pipeline has its West African agreement but not its investment decision.

How this page was made

The register
A list kept by hand in corridors.json, compiled on 12 Oct 2026 from governments', development banks' and companies' own pages and the trade press. Every piece, status and backer carries the address it was read from. Anything that could not be sourced was left out.
What counts
A corridor is in the register when a published priority list names it (the European Union's twelve Global Gateway strategic corridors for Africa, October 2023; the African Union's PIDA Priority Action Plan 2021–2030; the G7's PGII flagships), or when it is one of the continent's large cross-border routes with a foreign state behind it. One railway built for a mine is in, Simandou's, because Guinea's Trans-Guinean line is designed to carry passengers and other freight too. Corridors that exist only as lines on a regional plan, with no piece funded, are not.
Pieces
Each corridor is cut into the few pieces that make it: a port, a railway, a road, a pipeline, a border post. A corridor's own stage is that of its main line; each piece has its own.
Stage
The site's five: announced (an intention, a memorandum, a study), approved (a board's approval, or an agreement between governments, a concession or a financing signed, with no works yet), under construction, operating, stalled (halted for a year or more, cancelled or suspended).
Backers
Who lends, gives, takes equity, owns or holds the concession, and who builds. A state-owned company or policy bank counts for its state: China Eximbank and CCECC for China, DP World for the United Arab Emirates. Private companies and commercial banks are a group of their own. The map's "who pays" colour is each piece's first-named funder; for a piece still on paper that can be whoever paid for its studies.
Money
Costs and amounts are kept as the source states them, in its currency and for what it covers. They differ in date and scope, from a feasibility estimate to a signed loan, so the page adds none of them up.
