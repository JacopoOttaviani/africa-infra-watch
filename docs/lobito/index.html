Deep dive · Angola, DR Congo, Zambia

The Lobito Corridor

The Lobito Corridor is a railway, and so far only the old line carries its freight. The Benguela line runs 1,289 km from Angola's Atlantic port of Lobito to Luau on the Congolese border; since January 2024 a concession of Trafigura, Mota-Engil and Vecturis has run it, and from 2024 its trains have gone on along the DR Congo's own line to the copper and cobalt mines of Kolwezi. Around it, Washington, Brussels, Rome and the development banks have gathered roads, border posts, farms, power and studies under the corridor's name. This register holds 34 of them: 2 in service, 6 being built or carried out, 14 approved, 12 only announced.

USD 1.92 bn is signed or being paid out for those projects, USD 712 m has a board's approval and USD 1.71 bn is promised. The largest signed loan is the US Development Finance Corporation's USD 553 m to the railway, announced by Joe Biden and signed under Donald Trump. The headline figures are of another order: more than USD 4 bn of US investment, more than EUR 2 bn from Team Europe. They count private money and each other, and USD 2.65 bn of what the United States listed under the corridor's name pays for things away from it, mostly solar power elsewhere in Angola: more than everything signed for the corridor's own projects.

The next pieces are on paper. The DR Congo signed its line from Dilolo to Sakania to Mota-Engil in August 2026, with no loan yet for the works. The new railway into Zambia has the African Development Bank's approval but no builder, and its developer, the Africa Finance Corporation, aims to close its financing at the end of 2027.

Who runs it

Lobito Corridor Transit Transport Facilitation Agency (LCTTFA)
The three governments' agency, under SADC, to align transit and border rules along the corridor. The AfDB's 2026 package pays to get it working. Since January 2023. sadc.int
The 2023 memorandum
The US, the EU, Angola, the DR Congo, Zambia, the AfDB and the AFC set out who does what on the corridor and the Zambian railway; the Commission also lists Italy. Since October 2023. international-partnerships.ec.europa.eu
Lobito Atlantic Railway (LAR)
Trafigura, Mota-Engil and Vecturis: holds the Angolan railway and the mineral terminal for 30 years, runs trains into the DR Congo, borrows from the DFC and the DBSA. Since November 2022. engineeringnews.co.za
Africa Finance Corporation (AFC)
Developer of the new Zambian railway, with concessions from Angola and Zambia to finance, build, own and run it. Since October 2023. africafc.org
Mota-Engil Africa, in the DR Congo
Concessionaire of the Dilolo–Sakania railway for 30 years, the state keeping at least 10%; the Congolese railway SNCC keeps passenger rights. Since August 2026. logistafrica.com
Lobito Corridor Development Company (SDCL)
Angolan state company in Lobito to coordinate and promote investment along the corridor. Since January 2026. suica.mirex.gov.ao
Regional coordination meeting
The three governments and their funders, convened by Angola with the World Bank; first in Luanda, second in Lusaka on 5 October 2026. Since February 2026. governo.gov.ao

How this page was made

The register
A list kept by hand in lobito.json, compiled on 12 Oct 2026 from funders' and governments' own pages, company statements and the trade press. Every project, status and financing line carries the address it was read from. Anything that could not be sourced was left out.
What counts
A project is in the register when its funder or developer names the Lobito Corridor, or when it sits on the corridor's spine: the Benguela Railway from Lobito to Luau, the line on to Kolwezi, the planned line into Zambia and the roads and border posts that feed them. Mines are in only where a funder has put money into them under the corridor's name; otherwise they are the freight it was built for, not its projects.
Status
The site's five: announced, approved, under construction, operating, stalled. A project's status is its stage on the ground, not its money's.
Firmness
Kept apart from status, for each financing line: pledged (an announcement or a memorandum), approved (a board decision), signed (a loan, grant or concession agreement), disbursing (money flowing). The charts fold signed and disbursing together.
Not added up
The umbrella commitments (the United States' and the European Union's corridor-wide totals, Italy's pledge) are listed apart and never summed with the project lines. They overlap them, count private money their makers hope to attract and are restated from one summit to the next.
Lengths
As the concessionaires and governments state them: 1,289 km for the Benguela line (Lobito Atlantic Railway, MIGA), where older sources, the AidData record among them, give 1,344 km; about 1,000 km for the DR Congo line (the government), 1,037 km by Mota-Engil's count.
Currency
Amounts are kept in the currency the source states. Euros are converted to US dollars only to draw the charts, at 1 EUR = 1.1206 USD (ECB reference rate, 9 Oct 2026).
Map
Railways from OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), fetched 12 Oct 2026 through the Overpass API; borders, provinces, lakes, rivers and towns from Natural Earth 1:10m. A planned line is drawn through the places it is meant to link, as published, not along a surveyed route.
