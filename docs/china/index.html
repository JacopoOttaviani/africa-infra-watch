Lender profile · People's Republic of China

China

China publishes no project list, no aid statistics by recipient and nothing to the OECD or IATI; what exists is a reconstruction. AidData's Global Chinese Development Finance Dataset rebuilds every Chinese official commitment from public records, project by project, and the map already draws its infrastructure subset as triangles: 2,324 commitments to 52 African countries between 2000 and 2021, USD 242.7 bn in constant 2021 dollars, 1,242 of them loans worth USD 232.5 bn and 1,021 grants worth USD 10.0 bn. The China Export-Import Bank alone accounts for 57% of the money; Angola took USD 40.4 bn of it, more than any other country.

The other half of the record is what remains to be paid. The World Bank's debt statistics put public and publicly guaranteed debt owed to Chinese creditors at USD 82.2 bn at the end of 2024, across 46 governments, down from a peak of USD 91.9 bn in 2021; USD 62.2 bn of it is bilateral official lending, the rest Chinese commercial banks and other creditors. Angola owes the most, USD 14.4 bn. The map's own layers add 64 power units, 9 pipelines and 9 submarine cables with a Chinese owner.

Where it is on record

Commitments are summed at each country's interior point, sized by the twenty-one years of money; the 150 largest projects are drawn where AidData places them, in the map's status colours, and every one of the 2,324 is on the main map. CNPC's and CNOOC's pipelines are drawn as routes, the cables with a Chinese carrier as dotted lines, power units with a Chinese owner as diamonds. Debt owed to Chinese creditors is off until switched on.

A commitment and a debt are two views of the same money at different dates: the commitment is what was signed, in 2021 dollars; the debt is what is still owed, in current dollars, after drawdowns, repayments and restructurings. The two are shown side by side and never added. The 2Africa, SeaMeWe and PEACE cables run far beyond the frame and are cut at it.

Projects: AidData's reconstruction

AidData's method, Tracking Underreported Financial Flows, assembles each commitment from official documents, contracts, debt reports, press and company statements, and records what it could and could not establish: the lender, the implementer, the terms where known, the implementation status, and the amount, estimated where no figure was published (47 of the 2,324 records). The map's layer keeps the infrastructure subset as AidData flags it, and leaves out the 835 umbrella agreements whose money reappears in the projects beneath them, the 489 pledges and memoranda that AidData itself excludes from its totals, and the 5,674 commitments that are not physical infrastructure, so no dollar is counted twice and the layer has no announced records.

1,370 of the commitments are ODA-like, 741 other official flows at or near market terms; 169 were co-financed with other lenders, and AidData flags 174 as having gone into financial distress. By sector the largest is transport, USD 70.8 bn. Status: 1,603 operating, 498 under construction, 195 approved, 28 stalled.

Debt owed to Chinese creditors: World Bank International Debt Statistics

The debtors report; the World Bank publishes, by creditor country. For China the bilateral series, the one the other profiles use, misses the part that matters: the loans of Chinese commercial banks and other non-official creditors are filed under private creditors, not under the bilateral heading. The table therefore leads with all public and publicly guaranteed debt owed to Chinese creditors, and shows the bilateral official part beside it. Africa's stock owed to Chinese creditors rose from USD 2.7 bn in 2000 to a peak of USD 91.9 bn in 2021 and stood at USD 82.2 bn at the end of 2024.

How to read this page

One record is
In the projects table, one official commitment as AidData records it: one loan, grant or buyer's credit from one or more Chinese official lenders to one recipient; a railway financed in three tranches is three records. In the debt table, one country and one year. In the energy table, one GEM power unit, one pipeline (its segments counted) or one cable.
Status
AidData's implementation status maps as on the map: Pipeline: Commitment is approved, Implementation under construction, Completion operating, Suspended and Cancelled stalled; pledges are left out, so there are no announced records. Debt has no status.
Money
Commitments are AidData's figures in constant 2021 US dollars, estimated where marked. Debt is the World Bank's stock in current US dollars. The two describe the same money at different dates and are never added, to each other or to the map's other lenders.
Location
Country bubbles sit at the country's interior point. Projects use AidData's placement, stated in every tooltip: precise (on the OpenStreetMap feature), within 5 km, the centre of a named administrative area, or the country's interior point (428 records). Pipelines and cables are the map's routes, thinned; power units carry GEM's coordinates.
Not here, and why
  • Commitments after 2021. AidData's 3.0 release ends with 2021 commitments; the page and the map say so wherever the layer is described, and the debt series is the only part of the record that reaches 2024.
  • China's own statistics. The Ministry of Commerce publishes outward investment and contracting by country in an annual PDF bulletin, with no project level; the OECD holds no Chinese records; China does not publish to IATI.
  • Collations without a licence. The China Africa Research Initiative at Johns Hopkins redistributes the ministry's investment and contract-revenue figures as spreadsheets, and Boston University's Global Development Policy Center keeps a loan-by-loan database to 2023; neither states a reuse licence, so neither is on the site.
  • Oil and gas fields with Chinese operators are in GEM's Oil and Gas Extraction Tracker, released through a request form only; the pipelines are the open part.
