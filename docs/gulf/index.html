Lender profile · United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar

Gulf states

Four providers, read as one: the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Qatar, which report their aid to the OECD although none sits on its development committee. 6,010 activities with an African recipient for 2018–2024, USD 29.94 bn committed, 1,159 of them loans, the Saudi Fund's and the Kuwait Fund's; by provider, Saudi Arabia USD 16,309 m, United Arab Emirates USD 8,748 m, Kuwait USD 4,298 m, Qatar USD 587 m. The largest purpose is General budget support-related aid, USD 14,406 m; Egypt, Sudan and Tunisia receive the most.

The ground footprint is the newest on this site. The map's power layer holds 99 units owned by ACWA Power, AMEA Power, Masdar, TAQA, Phanes, Al Nowais or Alcazar, solar and wind from Egypt and Morocco to South Africa, Uganda and Togo, most of them built in the last five years; 2 pipeline segments carry Aramco or a Gulf company; and 18 cables land in Africa with a Gulf carrier among the owners. None of the four reports an investment stock to the OECD. What African governments owe the four states is USD 23.47 bn at the end of 2024, and USD 21.80 bn more to the Gulf-based development funds.

Aid activities: OECD Creditor Reporting System

The four report to the OECD at activity level as non-DAC providers: the Emirates' ministry and its foundations, the Saudi Fund for Development and the King Salman centre, the Kuwait Fund for Arab Economic Development, the Qatar Fund for Development and Qatar Charity. Together they give 2,759 distinct titles for 6,010 records. The loans are the Saudi and Kuwait funds' project lending for roads, dams, hospitals and power; the grants are budget support, humanitarian aid and the Emirates' and Qatar's charities' projects.

The purpose codes are the reliable part. The descriptions often name a region or a town (1,008 records mention one), but the CRS carries no coordinates, so the aid is drawn at country points and kept off the project map.

Country-year series: net aid and debt

Two official series describe Gulf money in Africa one country and one year at a time, and the second comes twice. The DAC2a table gives net ODA by recipient, summed over the four. The World Bank's debt statistics give what each government owes the four states, and, separately, what it owes the Gulf-based funds, the Arab Fund, the OPEC Fund, the Islamic Development Bank and BADEA, which lend on the same terms from the same capitals. There is no investment-stock series: none of the four reports to the OECD's FDI collection.

Over 2010–2024 net ODA from the Gulf states to Africa came to USD 24.01 bn; Egypt received the most.

How to read this page

One record is
In the aid tables, one CRS activity as each of the four reports it. In the energy table, one GEM power unit, one pipeline (its segments counted) or one cable, as the map carries them. In the series, one country and one year.
Status
GEM and TeleGeography records use the map's own mapping. Aid activities have no status.
Money
CRS commitments and disbursements are in current US dollars as reported. Net ODA is current prices. Debt is the World Bank's bilateral public and publicly guaranteed stock. None of these is a project value and none is added to the map's lenders.
Location
Aid and the series are drawn at the country's interior point. Pipelines and cables are the map's routes, thinned; power units carry GEM's coordinates.
Not here, and why
  • Investment stock by African country: none of the four reports to the OECD; the Emirates' and Saudi statistics publish Africa as a region, and the sovereign funds publish no portfolio by country.
  • DP World's and AD Ports' terminals, the largest Gulf footprint on the continent's coasts, are in none of the map's layers; a port layer would be the way to show them.
  • The Arab Coordination Group's project lists are the funds' own websites, one per fund, with no common machine-readable register.
  • Oil and gas fields with Gulf operators are in GEM's extraction tracker, released through a request form only.
