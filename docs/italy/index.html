Lender profile · Italian Republic

Italy

Italy is the one lender on this site with an official, placed project list. The Piano Mattei portal names 76 projects in 30 countries, with a stage, an implementer and a stated figure for 71 of them, EUR 4.35 bn in all; the map already draws them as hexagons. Behind the plan sits a full aid record, reported to the OECD at activity level with real titles: 10,068 Italian activities with an African recipient for 2018–2024, USD 4.99 bn committed, most of them not the government's own but the projects of religious bodies, NGOs and universities funded through Italy's tax-designation schemes.

The money that dwarfs both is private. Italian direct-investment stock in Africa stood at USD 29.9 bn at the end of 2024, Algeria and Egypt first, where Eni produces gas and oil; the map's own layers carry 26 pipelines with Eni, Snam or Edison among the owners, 29 power units owned by Enel, Eni, Building Energy or Renco, and 8 submarine cables with Sparkle. What each African government owes Italy is small by comparison: USD 1,682 m at the end of 2024.

Where it is on record

Piano Mattei projects sit where the map puts them: a named site or town where the portal's description gives one, otherwise the country's interior point, and one fixed point in the Central African Republic for programmes across several countries. Aid is drawn at each country's interior point, sized by seven years of commitments. Eni's pipelines are drawn as routes, Sparkle's cables as dotted lines, power units in the map's status colours. Debt owed to Italy is off until switched on.

The pipelines, power units and cables are the map's own records with an Italian name among the owners, Eni's share of a Nigerian or Libyan line included; the profile does not add records, it filters. Routes that leave the continent, the Trans-Mediterranean and Greenstream lines into Italy and the cables to the Gulf and India, are cut at the frame.

Piano Mattei: the Government's own list

The Presidency of the Council publishes one page per project, with the plan's directives, the objective, the countries, the implementing body, the partners, the funding source and a five-step progress bar. The map reads all of it and states, on every card, how precisely the project could be placed. 30 of the 76 projects are education and training; by stated money the largest sector is energy. Stages: 51 ongoing, 17 approved, 4 completed, 3 formulated, 1 identified.

The figures are the portal's own and are read as such: a stated amount may be the whole project's value, other funders' shares included, or Italy's contribution, and the portal does not say which. ELMED, the Tunisia–Sicily interconnector, carries EUR 1.4 bn that is mostly European and Tunisian money; a Climate Fund loan is Italy's alone. The amounts are therefore shown on a line of their own and never added to the lenders' commitments, here or on the map.

Aid activities: OECD Creditor Reporting System

Italy reports its aid to the OECD at activity level, with 7,768 distinct titles for 10,068 records over the seven years, descriptions that name partners and often towns, a purpose code, a channel and a finance type. What makes the record unusual is who reports it: 36% of the activities come under "Earmarked fiscal flows to NGOs & religious organisations", the tax-designation scheme through which taxpayers assign a share of their income tax, and a further large share from the NGO scheme and from public universities. The Agency for Development Cooperation, the regions and the foreign ministry report fewer, larger records. The biggest single lines are not projects at all: the 2021 cancellation of Somalia's debt under the HIPC initiative, the policy-based loans to Kenya and Rwanda, and the Italian Climate Fund's bond and equity subscriptions.

The purpose codes are the reliable part. The descriptions often name a town or a district (3,706 of the records mention one), but the CRS carries no coordinates and no gazetteer could be trusted at this scale, so the aid is drawn at country points and kept off the project map, as for the other lenders. Italy's IATI files, which carry locations for the agency's projects, are the natural next step and are not read yet.

Pipelines, power units and cables with an Italian owner

The map's energy and connectivity layers carry an owner field; this table is those records filtered for an Italian name. Eni's position is the largest of any Italian company on the continent: the Trans-Mediterranean line from Algeria through Tunisia, Greenstream from Libya, the Wafa–Mellitah and Sharara lines in Libya, the Zohr and Damietta lines in Egypt, the Brass and Tebidaba oil network in the Niger Delta, and a solar plant with Sonatrach at Bir Rebaa. Enel Green Power owns 1,032 MW of operating wind and solar, most of it in South Africa. Sparkle, Telecom Italia's international arm, sits in the ownership of 8 cables that land in Africa.

Country-year series: investment stock, net aid, debt

Three official series describe Italian money in Africa one country and one year at a time. The OECD's FDI positions give Italy's outward direct-investment stock by partner country, which is where the oil and gas is: Algeria, Egypt and Nigeria lead. The DAC2a table gives net ODA by recipient. The World Bank's debt statistics give what each government owes Italy, which is what remains of decades of bilateral lending after the debt relief of the 2000s.

How to read this page

One record is
In the Piano Mattei table, one project page on the Government's portal. In the aid tables, one CRS activity as Italy reports it. In the energy table, one GEM power unit, one pipeline (its segments counted) or one cable, as the map carries them. In the series, one country and one year.
Status
Piano Mattei stages map as on the map: identified and formulated are announced, approved is approved, ongoing is under construction, completed is operating. GEM and TeleGeography records use the map's own mapping. Aid activities have no status.
Money
Piano Mattei amounts are the portal's figures in euros, shown as stated. CRS commitments and disbursements are in current US dollars as reported. Net ODA is current prices. FDI stock is the OECD's net position, all resident units, immediate counterpart. Debt is the World Bank's bilateral public and publicly guaranteed stock. None of these is added to the map's lenders, and none is added to the others.
Location
Piano Mattei projects use the map's placement: a site or town named in the description, a named administrative area, the country's interior point, or the plan's fixed continental point. Aid and the three series are drawn at the country's interior point. Pipelines and cables are the map's routes, thinned; power units carry GEM's coordinates.
Not here, and why
  • Italy's IATI files (the Agency for Development Cooperation and the foreign ministry publish them) carry locations and documents for the agency's projects; the site does not read them yet. They would be the richest addition.
  • SACE, the export-credit agency, publishes no register of insured or guaranteed transactions; Cassa Depositi e Prestiti publishes no project list for its lending in Africa. Where either shows up, it is through the CRS loan records or the Piano Mattei pages.
  • Eni's and others' oil and gas fields are in GEM's Oil and Gas Extraction Tracker, released through a request form only.
  • The OECD's bulk CRS files sit behind a browser challenge; the page uses the SDMX microdata query, one year at a time.
