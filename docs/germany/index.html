Lender profile · Federal Republic of Germany

Germany

Germany's record is the Federal Ministry for Economic Cooperation and Development's, delivered by KfW and GIZ, and it reports it in full. 28,650 activities with an African recipient for 2018–2024, USD 48.02 bn committed, 291 of them loans, KfW's development loans to the larger economies; 54% of the activities are reported by the Federal Ministry for Economic Cooperation and Development, the rest by the research, foreign and economic affairs ministries and by DEG, the development finance arm. The largest purpose is Formal sector financial intermediaries, USD 4,827 m; Morocco, Tunisia and Egypt receive the most.

German direct-investment stock in Africa stood at USD 14.7 bn at the end of 2024, South Africa 48% of it, the carmakers' plants; the map's own power layer carries 12 units owned by juwi, ib vogt, BayWa r.e. or Siemens, most of them solar farms in South Africa. What African governments owe Germany is USD 9,304 m at the end of 2024, Egypt first, mostly KfW's development loans and what survived the debt relief of the 2000s.

Where it is on record

Aid is drawn at each country's interior point, sized by seven years of commitments; the CRS carries no coordinates, so a country point is the honest place. Power units with a German owner sit at Global Energy Monitor's coordinates in the map's status colours. Debt owed to Germany is off until switched on.

Germany's IATI files, which KfW and GIZ fill with locations, documents and results for the ministry's projects, are the one source that would put German aid on the project map. The site does not read them yet, and says so below.

Aid activities: OECD Creditor Reporting System

Germany reports to the OECD at activity level, with 12,185 distinct titles for 28,650 records and descriptions that name the partner institution and often the region. The ministry's bilateral programmes are the bulk; the loans are KfW's, to Egypt, Morocco, Tunisia, Kenya and the other economies it lends to on its own terms with a federal subsidy; the research ministry's many small records are university partnerships; DEG's are equity and loans to companies, reported as private-sector instruments.

The purpose codes are the reliable part. The descriptions often name a region or a town (10,746 records mention one), but the CRS carries no coordinates, so the aid is drawn at country points and kept off the project map.

Country-year series: investment stock, net aid, debt

Three official series describe German money in Africa one country and one year at a time. The OECD's FDI positions, which for Germany are the Bundesbank's figures, give the outward direct-investment stock by partner country. The DAC2a table gives net ODA by recipient. The World Bank's debt statistics give what each government owes Germany: KfW's development loans and the older claims that survived the debt relief of the 2000s.

How to read this page

One record is
In the aid tables, one CRS activity as Germany reports it. In the energy table, one GEM power unit, as the map carries it. In the series, one country and one year.
Status
GEM records use the map's own mapping. Aid activities have no status.
Money
CRS commitments and disbursements are in current US dollars as reported. Net ODA is current prices. FDI stock is the OECD's net position, all resident units, immediate counterpart. Debt is the World Bank's bilateral public and publicly guaranteed stock. None of these is a project value and none is added to the map's lenders.
Location
Aid and the three series are drawn at the country's interior point. Power units carry GEM's coordinates.
Not here, and why
  • Germany's IATI files: the ministry publishes one file for all its implementers, with KfW's and GIZ's project locations, documents and results. The site does not read it yet; it would be the richest single addition to the project map among the bilateral providers.
  • DEG's portfolio is published as a list of companies and countries without amounts per investment; Hermes export-credit guarantees have no project register.
  • The federal government's Compact with Africa and its Africa strategy are policy documents, not datasets.
