Lender profile · Japan

Japan

Japan is the lender that builds: JICA's yen loans for ports, roads, power and water are the spine of its record. 32,502 activities with an African recipient for 2018–2024, USD 14.31 bn committed, 146 of them loans; 88% of the activities are reported by the Japanese International Co-operation Agency. The largest purpose is Road transport, USD 1,767 m; Egypt, Kenya and Morocco receive the most. 7,620 records, USD 7,070 m, build, renovate or drill something, a larger share than for any other provider on this site.

The corporate footprint is thinner than the lending. Japan reports its direct-investment stock in Africa for South Africa alone, USD 4.5 bn at the end of 2024; the map's own layers carry 9 power units owned by Eurus Energy, Toyota Tsusho or Sumitomo, wind and solar in Egypt, Kenya and South Africa, and 1 pipeline segment with Sojitz among the owners. What African governments owe Japan is USD 8,317 m at the end of 2024, Egypt first: the yen loans themselves.

Where it is on record

Aid is drawn at each country's interior point, sized by seven years of commitments; the CRS carries no coordinates, so a country point is the honest place. Power units with a Japanese owner sit at Global Energy Monitor's coordinates in the map's status colours. Debt owed to Japan is off until switched on.

JICA's own project map and its IATI files place the yen-loan projects; the site does not read them yet, so the loans sit at country points here and the works they paid for appear on the main map only where another source holds them.

Aid activities: OECD Creditor Reporting System

Japan reports to the OECD at activity level, with 2,640 distinct titles for 32,502 records: JICA's loans, grants and technical cooperation, the foreign ministry's grassroots grants, the ministries' and universities' smaller lines. The loans are the money: a port in Mombasa or Nacala, a bridge, a thermal or geothermal plant, a water system, each a single record of several hundred million dollars with the project named in the title. The grants are the count.

The purpose codes are the reliable part. The descriptions often name a region or a town (1,453 records mention one), but the CRS carries no coordinates, so the aid is drawn at country points and kept off the project map.

The OECD's FDI positions carry a Japanese figure for South Africa only; Japan's own statistics publish Africa as one aggregate.

How to read this page

One record is
In the aid tables, one CRS activity as Japan reports it. In the energy table, one GEM power unit, one pipeline (its segments counted) or one cable, as the map carries them. In the series, one country and one year.
Status
GEM and TeleGeography records use the map's own mapping. Aid activities have no status.
Money
CRS commitments and disbursements are in current US dollars as reported. Net ODA is current prices. FDI stock is the OECD's net position, immediate counterpart, where Japan reports it. Debt is the World Bank's bilateral public and publicly guaranteed stock. None of these is a project value and none is added to the map's lenders.
Location
Aid and the series are drawn at the country's interior point. Pipelines and cables are the map's routes, thinned; power units carry GEM's coordinates.
Not here, and why
  • JICA's IATI files and project map carry locations for the yen-loan and grant projects; the site does not read them yet. They would put Japanese aid on the project map more precisely than any other provider's.
  • JBIC's and NEXI's export finance to private borrowers has no project register.
  • Japan's FDI by African country: the Bank of Japan and JETRO publish Africa as one aggregate; the OECD holds a figure for South Africa only.
  • Submarine cables: the map's cable layer names no Japanese owner on an African landing.
