Lender profile · Russian Federation

Russia

There is no Russian counterpart to AidData's project-level record of Chinese finance. Russia reports totals, not projects, to the OECD, and nothing to IATI. What is on record is narrower, and this page holds all of it: what each African government owes the Russian Federation, year by year, from the World Bank's debt statistics; the four energy assets in Global Energy Monitor's trackers with a Russian vendor or shareholder; the 15 countries with a Rosatom agreement; and the Bank of Russia's thin table of investment stocks, which blanks out the countries that matter. Everything else is press, and is listed as press.

The sources agree on one picture. Egypt's El Dabaa nuclear plant is almost the whole of Russian official money in Africa this decade: a USD 25 bn state export loan signed in 2015, drawing down since 2019. Soviet-era claims of about USD 15.6 bn in 1991 were written down to USD 0.45 bn by 2011; the stock has climbed back to USD 4.49 bn at the end of 2024, and Egypt owes 91% of it.

Sovereign loans: World Bank International Debt Statistics

The IDS publishes every low- and middle-income borrower's external debt by individual creditor, from the borrowers' own reporting to the World Bank's Debtor Reporting System. The Russian Federation is counterpart 087; the USSR, for claims made before 1992, is 078. For each African country and year it gives the stock owed, new commitments, disbursements and repayments, from 1970 to 2024. It is open (CC BY 4.0), machine-readable through the same World Bank API the map's other fetchers use, and refreshed every December.

What it cannot say is what a loan paid for. The purpose column below is filled only where a public source says so: El Dabaa, Angola's satellite. It also sees only sovereign and sovereign-guaranteed borrowing, so Rusal's mines, Lukoil's blocks and VTB's 2013 loans to Mozambican state companies are invisible here.

Stocks that end before 2024 are countries that stopped reporting a Russian balance, usually after a write-off: Russia cancelled Soviet-era claims on Ethiopia (USD 5.5 bn, 2005), Algeria (2006), Libya and most of the heavily indebted poor countries. The IDS records a cancellation as the stock going to zero, not as a flow. The Soviet-era column is the peak stock owed to the USSR under counterpart 078.

Energy assets with a Russian role: Global Energy Monitor

Four records in GEM's trackers carry a Russian vendor, owner or parent. Two of them are already on the map, in the power and pipelines layers. The map's power layer takes GEM's Integrated Power Tracker; the Nuclear Power Tracker's August 2026 release adds the reactor model, which is how El Dabaa shows up as Russian at all: GEM books its owner as Egypt's Nuclear Power Plants Authority, and the Russian role is in the model (VVER-1200) and on the wiki page.

GEM's Oil and Gas Extraction Tracker would add the upstream stakes (Lukoil in Ghana, the Republic of the Congo, Egypt and Cameroon; Gazprom in Algeria; Rosneft in Mozambique and Egypt; Tatneft in Libya), but it is released only through a request form, not the open bucket the fetcher reads, so it is not here.

Nuclear agreements: World Nuclear Association

The WNA's Emerging nuclear energy countries profile is the one maintained, dated record of Rosatom's African agreements. It is a narrative page, not a dataset, and almost every entry is a memorandum with no money, no site and no date of works. Only Egypt has a contract and a construction site. Read as announced records at country level they are honest; read as investment they are not.

How to read this page

One record is
In the debt table, one country and its balance with the Russian Federation by year. In the assets table, one GEM record (a plant or a pipeline segment). In the agreements table, one country's relationship with Rosatom, however many memoranda it spans. In the holdings table, one company stake as a named source describes it.
Status
GEM assets use the map's five statuses, mapped as on the map. Rosatom agreements are announced records, whatever a memorandum calls itself; only El Dabaa is under construction. Debt balances have no status: a loan is approved when committed, disbursing while drawn, and then repaid, and the table shows the money rather than a label.
Money
Debt figures are the World Bank's, in current US dollars, public and publicly guaranteed debt to creditors in Russia, the state and its banks together, as the debt page counts it; the bilateral official part, the state's own claims, is in the table beside it. They are balances, not project values, and are never added to the map's lenders: a debt stock is what is still owed, a commitment is what was signed, and the two are shown side by side rather than summed. Bank of Russia stocks are USD million, directional principle, as the Bank publishes them.
Location
Debt, agreements and holdings are drawn at the country's interior point, the same point the map uses for records it can place no finer than the country. GEM assets sit at GEM's coordinates (exact for El Dabaa and Lurio, approximate for Itu, a route for the pipeline).
Not here, and why
  • GEM's Oil and Gas Extraction Tracker, which holds the upstream stakes, is released through a request form only.
  • GIGA's greenfield FDI dataset (11,233 projects in Africa 2013–2020 with investor origin) needs a GESIS login and a licence check before any row could be published.
  • OECD development-finance statistics hold no Russian records at recipient level: the query for Russia as a provider returns nothing.
  • AidData and IATI have no Russian dataset; Russia does not publish to IATI.
  • Think-tank reports (IFRI, CSIS, GIS Reports) cite UNCTAD, SIPRI and fDi Markets totals, with no data annex; they put Russia under 1% of foreign direct investment in Africa.
