Deep dive · Kenya, Ethiopia, South Sudan

The LAPSSET Corridor

LAPSSET is a port that works and a corridor that mostly does not exist yet. Kenya, Ethiopia and South Sudan launched it in March 2012 as a plan of about USD 25 bn: a port at Lamu, with highways, a railway, oil pipelines, airports and resort cities running north-west to Juba and north to Addis Ababa. So far it has produced three berths at Lamu, opened in 2021 and 2022 and busy only since 2025, much of their cargo diverted by the conflict in the Gulf, and two paved highways to the borders, Isiolo–Moyale and Lokichar–Nadapal, built with African Development Bank, EU and World Bank money. This register holds 40 of the corridor's parts: 12 in service, 3 being built, 3 approved, 15 only announced and 7 stalled.

USD 2.16 bn is signed or being paid out for those projects, less than a tenth of the USD 24.5 bn the corridor authority put on the plan in 2016, and most of it from Kenya's own budget, the African Development Bank and the World Bank. Kenya pays in shillings for the port and for the road from Lamu to Garissa, both built by China Communications Construction Company. USD 532 m more is promised, almost all of it a proposed Kenyan stake in a refinery.

The rest is on paper. The railway has no money for construction. The crude pipeline from the Turkana oilfields to Lamu was left out of the oil plan Parliament ratified in February 2026, which trucks the crude to Mombasa. On 1 October 2026 President Ruto said Kenya would build one with Aliko Dangote, whose USD 16 bn refinery had broken ground at Lamu the day before. In South Sudan the road from the border to Juba has stalled, and in January 2025 Kenya's cabinet approved dissolving the authority that coordinates the corridor.

Who runs it

LAPSSET Corridor Development Authority (LCDA)
Kenyan state corporation set up by the LCDA Order (Legal Notice 58 of 2013, State Corporations Act) to plan, coordinate and sequence LAPSSET projects with implementing ministries and agencies, and to mobilise funds (budget, donor loans, infrastructure bonds, private finance). HQ is in Nairobi with field offices in Lamu, Isiolo, Lokichogio, Marsabit and Moyale. The Order lists 13 'Projects Components', including a merchant oil refinery at Lamu. How this page was made

The register
A list kept by hand in lapsset.json, compiled on 12 Oct 2026 from the LAPSSET Corridor Development Authority's, funders' and governments' own pages, company statements and the press. Every project, status and financing line carries the address it was read from. Anything that could not be sourced was left out.
What counts
A project is in the register when the LAPSSET Corridor Development Authority lists it as a component (on its website or in the 2013 order that created it), when its funder or developer names LAPSSET, or when it is a section of one of the corridor's roads, its railway or its pipelines. Roads that reach the corridor from elsewhere (Eldoret–Lokichar, Isiolo–Mandera), Ethiopia's expressway north of Hawassa, where the corridor's road ends, and the oilfields the pipeline was meant to serve are under "Looked at and left out", each with the reason.
Status
The site's five: announced, approved, under construction, operating, stalled. A project's status is its stage on the ground, not its money's.
Firmness
Kept apart from status, for each financing line: pledged (an announcement or a memorandum), approved (a board decision), signed (a loan, grant, contract or budget allocation), disbursing (money flowing). The charts fold signed and disbursing together.
Not added up
The corridor-wide figures (the cost of the whole plan, investment conference pledges, memoranda) are listed apart and never summed with the project lines. They overlap them and are restated from one speech to the next.
Currency
Amounts are kept in the currency the source states. Euros are converted to US dollars only to draw the charts, at 1 EUR = 1.1206 USD (ECB reference rate, 9 Oct 2026). Kenyan shillings, the currency of most of Kenya's own spending on the corridor, are converted at the official rate's annual average for the year of the commitment, from the World Bank's World Development Indicators (indicator PA.NUS.FCRF, annual averages to 2025, read on 12 October 2026; 2026 lines use the 2025 rate), since the shilling lost a third of its value against the dollar between 2014 and 2024.
Map
Roads and railways from OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), fetched 12 Oct 2026 through the Overpass API: the Lamu–Garissa–Isiolo highway is road A10 and the ways tagged LAPSSET, Isiolo–Moyale the A2, the road to South Sudan the A1 north of Lokichar. The crude oil pipelines' routes are Global Energy Monitor's; in its file the two LAPSSET segments carry each other's route, so each is drawn by where it starts. Borders, counties, lakes, rivers and towns from Natural Earth 1:10m. A planned line without a published route is drawn through the places it is meant to link.
