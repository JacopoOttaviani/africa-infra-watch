How to read this — sources, method and the limits that matter

The three layers

No single dataset covers African infrastructure. This consolidates three that complement each other: who is paying (African Development Bank activity files), what asset is being built (Global Energy Monitor's power tracker), and what has physically broken ground (OpenStreetMap construction tags).

They share no project identifier, so they are shown as three map layers over a common country spine rather than merged into one table of "projects". Counting across layers would double-count: an AfDB-financed power plant can legitimately appear in all three.

One status vocabulary

Each source's own lifecycle is mapped onto five shared values:

  • Announced — GEM announced, IATI status 1, OSM proposed
  • Approved — GEM pre-construction; AfDB projects past board approval
  • Under construction — GEM construction, IATI status 2, OSM construction
  • Operating — GEM operating, IATI status 3–4
  • Stalled — GEM cancelled/shelved/mothballed, IATI 5–6

Limits you should carry into any conclusion

  • Money is AfDB only. Commitments come from AfDB transaction records (IATI type 2), converted from SDR at a fixed published rate. Chinese, private, domestic-budget and other-lender finance is not here — treat totals as "AfDB exposure", never "investment in Africa".
  • Roughly two-thirds of AfDB points are approximate. Locations flagged exactness=2 sit on a country or region centroid, not the works. The map prefers an exact point where one exists; where it doesn't, expect a marker near the country's centre. The table shows which.
  • OSM presence is mapper-driven. Absence of a road here is not evidence no road is being built. It is also filtered to significant classes — motorway, trunk, primary, secondary, all rail and airport works — because an unfiltered query is dominated by residential street stubs inside housing estates.
  • Energy is over-represented. GEM covers power comprehensively; no comparable open tracker exists for ports, water or ICT, so those appear only where AfDB finances them or OSM mappers trace them.

Provenance

Country outlines are Natural Earth 1:110m. Five small island states — Cabo Verde, São Tomé and Príncipe, Comoros, Mauritius and Seychelles — are below that resolution and are drawn as markers, so their projects sit on a marker rather than a coastline. The projection is plain equirectangular: undistorted near the equator, which is where most of this continent sits, and progressively stretched north and south.

Natural Earth draws Somaliland and Western Sahara as polygons separate from Somalia and Morocco. That is the boundary dataset's cartographic choice, not a position taken here; works falling inside those outlines are attributed to them, which is why the territory count exceeds the 54 UN member states.
