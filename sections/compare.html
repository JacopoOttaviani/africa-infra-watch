Where the money goes

One small map per lender, the same measure on each. With share of the lender's total, the darkest country on every map is that lender's largest counterpart, so the maps compare priorities; with USD, one scale, they compare size, and a small lender's map goes pale. Switch to who leads where to colour each country by the lender with the largest figure there.

Focused or spread

How much of each lender's figure sits in its three largest counterparts, and how many countries it reaches at all. A high bar is a lender with a few favourites; a low one, a lender that is everywhere.

What they fund

Each row is one lender's aid commitments split by the DAC's sector headings, as a share of its total; darker is more. Read across a row for a lender's priorities, down a column for who cares about a sector. The sources do not split purposes by country, so this grid is Africa-wide whatever the scope above.

How to read this page

What is compared
The seven lenders with a profile on this site, each on the measures its profile already holds. Nothing is fetched for this page: it re-cuts the profiles' packs and the debt pack, so a figure here is the same figure there. Owed to the lender is the World Bank's International Debt Statistics series by creditor (DT.DOD.DPPG.CD): every African government's public and publicly guaranteed external debt to that lending country, its government and its banks together, at the end of each year, __DEBT_FROM__–__DEBT_TO__. The European Union is the European Investment Bank plus the EU budget, the European Development Fund and the old EEC loans. __MISSING__ do not report to the World Bank and have no creditor figures.
Aid committed
For Italy, Turkey, Germany, the United States and the EU institutions: every activity with an African recipient in the OECD's Creditor Reporting System, __AID_FROM__–__AID_TO__, commitments in current dollars (disbursements where no commitment is stated). For China: AidData's Global Chinese Development Finance Dataset 3.0, official commitments to infrastructure projects only, __CHINA_FROM__–__CHINA_TO__, in constant 2021 dollars. The two are not the same thing and the page never adds them: with USD, one scale China's maps share a colour scale with the others only so their sizes can be seen together. Russia publishes no activity-level aid data.
Net ODA and FDI
OECD DAC2a net disbursements by recipient (measure 206, current prices), __ODA_FROM__–__ODA_TO__, for the five OECD reporters; OECD outward direct-investment positions by partner country (BMD4, immediate counterpart), __FDI_FROM__–__FDI_TO__, for Italy, Turkey, the United States and Germany. The EU institutions have no FDI reporter; China and Russia publish neither series in this form.
Share of the lender's total
On the maps, each country's figure divided by the lender's sum over the countries in scope, so every lender's largest counterpart is drawn at full strength. USD, one scale uses the largest single figure among the lenders shown instead. The sector and channel grids are always shares of a lender's whole African total: the sources do not break purposes or channels down by country.
Who leads where
Each country takes the colour and the badge of the lender, among those selected, with the largest figure there for the measure and year chosen. A country where none of the selected lenders has a figure above zero stays uncoloured.
Sectors
DAC purpose codes folded into the DAC's own headings by their leading digits: 11 education, 12 and 13 health and population, 14 water, 15 government and civil society, 16 other social, 21 transport, 22 communications, 23 energy, 24, 25 and 33 banking, business and trade, 31 agriculture, 32 industry and mining, 41 and 43 environment and multisector, 51 to 53 and 60 budget support, food aid and debt relief, 7 humanitarian. The packs keep each lender's fourteen largest purposes, which cover __COVER_NOTE__ commitments; the remainder is one column. AidData's own sector names are mapped onto the same headings for China.
Builds something
The share of a lender's commitments in the "hard" subset the profiles define: purpose codes 14, 21, 22 and 23 (water, transport, communications, energy) plus education facilities and basic health infrastructure, or a title or description that says construct, renovate, rehabilitate, drill, road, bridge, hospital, clinic, irrigation. AidData's dataset is infrastructure by definition, so China's share is not computed.
Grants and loans
Shares of each lender's aid by the OECD's measure (ODA grants, ODA loans, other official flows, equity and private-sector instruments). A pack fetched after October 2026 carries the money by measure and the share is of dollars; an older pack carries only the count of activities, and the row says so. China's split is AidData's loans and grants in dollars.
Colours
One hue per lender, fixed on every chart, from a categorical order checked for colour-blind separation of neighbours in both themes; China, the European Union and the United States keep the hues of the debt section's creditor groups. No mark relies on colour alone: every bar, line end and country carries the lender's name or badge, and the table restates everything. The grids use one hue, darker for more.
