Deep dive · Angola, DR Congo, Zambia

The Lobito Corridor

The Lobito Corridor is a railway, and so far only the old line carries its freight. The Benguela line runs 1,289 km from Angola's Atlantic port of Lobito to Luau on the Congolese border; since January 2024 a concession of Trafigura, Mota-Engil and Vecturis has run it, and from 2024 its trains have gone on along the DR Congo's own line to the copper and cobalt mines of Kolwezi. Around it, Washington, Brussels, Rome and the development banks have gathered roads, border posts, farms, power and studies under the corridor's name. This register holds __N_PROJECTS__ of them: __N_OPERATING__ in service, __N_BUILDING__ being built or carried out, __N_APPROVED__ approved, __N_ANNOUNCED__ only announced.

__SIGNED__ is signed or being paid out for those projects, __APPROVED__ has a board's approval and __PLEDGED__ is promised. The largest signed loan is the US Development Finance Corporation's USD 553 m to the railway, announced by Joe Biden and signed under Donald Trump. The headline figures are of another order: more than USD 4 bn of US investment, more than EUR 2 bn from Team Europe. They count private money and each other, and __ELSEWHERE__ of what the United States listed under the corridor's name pays for things away from it, mostly solar power elsewhere in Angola: __ELSEWHERE_CMP__ everything signed for the corridor's own projects.

The next pieces are on paper. The DR Congo signed its line from Dilolo to Sakania to Mota-Engil in August 2026, with no loan yet for the works. The new railway into Zambia has the African Development Bank's approval but no builder, and its developer, the Africa Finance Corporation, aims to close its financing at the end of 2027.

How this page was made

The register
A list kept by hand in lobito.json, compiled on __COMPILED__ from funders' and governments' own pages, company statements and the trade press. Every project, status and financing line carries the address it was read from. Anything that could not be sourced was left out.
What counts
A project is in the register when its funder or developer names the Lobito Corridor, or when it sits on the corridor's spine: the Benguela Railway from Lobito to Luau, the line on to Kolwezi, the planned line into Zambia and the roads and border posts that feed them. Mines are in only where a funder has put money into them under the corridor's name; otherwise they are the freight it was built for, not its projects.
Status
The site's five: announced, approved, under construction, operating, stalled. A project's status is its stage on the ground, not its money's.
Firmness
Kept apart from status, for each financing line: pledged (an announcement or a memorandum), approved (a board decision), signed (a loan, grant or concession agreement), disbursing (money flowing). The charts fold signed and disbursing together.
Not added up
The umbrella commitments (the United States' and the European Union's corridor-wide totals, Italy's pledge) are listed apart and never summed with the project lines. They overlap them, count private money their makers hope to attract and are restated from one summit to the next.
Lengths
As the concessionaires and governments state them: 1,289 km for the Benguela line (Lobito Atlantic Railway, MIGA), where older sources, the AidData record among them, give 1,344 km; about 1,000 km for the DR Congo line (the government), 1,037 km by Mota-Engil's count.
Currency
Amounts are kept in the currency the source states. Euros are converted to US dollars only to draw the charts, at __EUR_NOTE__.
Map
Railways from OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), fetched __RAIL_FETCHED__ through the Overpass API; borders, provinces, lakes, rivers and towns from Natural Earth 1:10m. A planned line is drawn through the places it is meant to link, as published, not along a surveyed route.
