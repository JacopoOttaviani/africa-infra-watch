How this page was made

Production
The British Geological Survey's World Mineral Statistics, through its open API, for mine production by country from __BGS_FIRST__ to __BGS_YEAR__. It names every producing country, so Africa's share is a whole share. Where a country reports a total and its parts (gem and industrial diamonds, grades of ore), the total is taken. Contains British Geological Survey materials © UKRI 2026, used with the acknowledgement BGS asks for. For lithium, tantalum and rare earths, whose BGS tables mix ores, concentrates and contained metal country by country, the USGS's __MCS_YEAR__ figures are used instead.
Reserves
The USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 (public domain), read from its "World Mine Production and Reserves" tables. The USGS folds smaller producers into "other countries" and leaves some reserves unreported, so Africa's shares from it are floors.
Mines and plants
The USGS Compilation of Geospatial Data (GIS) for the Mineral Industries and Related Infrastructure of Africa (2021): every mine and processing plant in the USGS Minerals Yearbook's African country chapters for 2018, located, with its commodities, capacity, status, operator and equity owners. A site listed once per commodity is drawn once. Oil and gas fields and refineries are left out. Status and owners are as of 2018. Its exploration layer (the USGS World Minerals Exploration Database) is the map's third layer.
Owners
The owners' home countries are this page's classification, not the USGS's: each owner counts for the home country of its ultimate parent in 2018, from the parent's filings and listing; a government or company of the mine's own country counts as "host". The full list is in the review table under the chart, and in minerals_owners.json. A mine's equity is split by the stakes the USGS gives; owners listed without a stake share what is left.
Deposits
Two USGS datasets from its ArcGIS server: Open-File Report 2005-1294 (major mineral deposits of Africa and the Middle East) and Professional Paper 1802 (critical-mineral deposits of the world, 2017). Each point is placed in a country by its coordinates; a deposit listed in both, within about 8 km and with a mineral in common, is drawn once.
Trade
UN Comtrade's public API: imports reported by every other country in __TRADE_YEAR__ from each African country that mines the mineral (per the BGS and USGS), for the HS headings of its ores and first products (listed in the table). Values are at the importer's border, in US dollars; goods refined or re-exported in a country count as that country's. A country that has not reported a year is missing from it: in __TRADE_YEAR__ that is the United Arab Emirates, whose last reported year is given beside the chart and never added in. Lithium's heading, 2530.90, also holds other minerals, so its row is approximate. Rare earths are left out: Africa's concentrates ship under headings shared with other ores.
Rare earths
A register kept by hand in minerals_ree.json: each project with its owners and their home countries, its stage, resource, buyers and lenders, and the address each fact was read from: ministries, regulators' filings (ASX, TSX, LSE, JSE), technical reports and development banks' own pages.
Files
minerals.json (production, reserves, trade), minerals_sites.geojson (deposits), minerals_facilities.geojson (mines and plants) and minerals_exploration.geojson. Rebuilt with fetch_minerals.py and build_minerals.py.
